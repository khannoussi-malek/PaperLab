Explain the paper to a curious reader from outside its field.

Cover, in this order:

1. The problem: what was not known or not possible before, and why anyone wanted it.
2. What they did: the approach in ordinary words, with one concrete example if the paper gives one.
3. What they found: the main results, with the key numbers as the paper states them.
4. How strong the evidence is: how much data, how many comparisons, and how sure the authors themselves are.
5. Why it might matter to someone outside the subfield.

Define each piece of jargon once, in plain words, the first time it appears, then use it consistently. Keep the result exactly as strong as the paper makes it: don't turn "improved on two benchmarks" into "solves", and keep the authors' hedges. If part of the paper is too technical to explain without losing accuracy, say that part briefly and plainly rather than guessing.

End with a two-sentence summary the reader could keep as a note.
